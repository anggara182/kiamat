The Beginning of the End

Phase 01 · The World We Know

One ordinary day in a modern city, dawn to night. The minor signs described fourteen centuries ago sit quietly inside everyday life.

“They ask you about the Hour: when is its arrival? Say, ‘Its knowledge is only with my Lord.’”

Qur’an 7:187 · Sahih International
Narration

The world doesn't end with a bang. It ends slowly… one ordinary morning at a time.

This city wakes before the sun. Kitchen lights flicker on. Engines cough awake. The call to prayer rises over a hundred phone alarms.

By seven, the streets are full. Millions of people. One goal: survive one more day.

The towers keep rising. From the top, the people below are just dots.

The news moves faster now. An earthquake here. A war there. Whole peoples wiped out, while the world watches. A life taken over almost nothing. A man trusted with everything… betrays it all.

Read. Swipe. Forgotten.

And the days feel shorter. Morning starts… and suddenly, it's night again.

Nothing seems wrong. But all of this, towers racing for the sky, trust placed in the wrong hands, the earth shaking again and again, killing that no longer shocks anyone, time slipping away, was foretold by the Prophet Muhammad, fourteen hundred years ago.

When the end will come, no one knows. Only God.

Night falls like any other night. The lights go out, one by one.

Tomorrow morning, a message will spread through the entire city… about something that never happened.

Caption
The world doesn't end with a bang. It ends one ordinary morning at a time.

Sources & classification
  • Competing in tall buildings: Sahih Muslim 8; Sahih al-Bukhari 50, 7121
  • Trust neglected, authority given to the unqualified: Sahih al-Bukhari 59
  • Earthquakes, killing (harj), time passing quickly, two great groups fighting: Sahih al-Bukhari 7121
  • Only God knows the Hour: Qur’an 7:187, 31:34
Canonical
The conditions named in the recap line and the statement that only God knows the time.
Interpretation
“A war there. Whole peoples wiped out” read as part of harj (much killing) and fighting in Bukhari 7121; “genocide” is modern language, not a hadith term. The opening line is the series’ storytelling voice, not a claim about timing.
Fiction
All people, the city, every headline (generic, no real events, places or organisations), the notifications and the false message at the end.

Faster Than the Truth

Phase 01 → 02 · The Age of Fitnah begins

A rumour that the water is poisoned spreads mouth to mouth and nearly ends with a crowd stoning an innocent newcomer. The water was clean all along.

“Believers, if a troublemaker brings you news, check it first, in case you wrong others unwittingly and later regret what you have done.”

Qur’an 49:6 · Abdel Haleem
Narration

By sunrise, the whole city knew.

The only problem… none of it was true.

It started as one whisper at a food stall.

The water is poisoned.

Someone saw a stranger by the water tower last night.

Nobody asked who saw him.

Nobody asked how they knew.

By seven, the shelves were empty.

Mothers poured water down the drain.

Old men argued at the mosque gate.

And the story grew with every mouth it passed through.

One stranger became three. Poison became a plan.

By noon, they found him.

A young man, new in the neighborhood.

He had been fixing a leaking pipe… for free.

The crowd closed in.

Someone picked up a stone.

Then the mosque speaker crackled.

The water had been tested. It was clean. It always had been.

The crowd went home.

No one said sorry. No one asked who started it.

The warning had been written long ago.

And the Prophet said that repeating everything you hear is lie enough.

That night, a new whisper began. This time, it named someone everyone trusted.

Caption
By sunrise, the whole city knew. None of it was true.

The Man From Nowhere

Stage 2 · Fitnah & false claimants

A rumour sets a whole city on fire. The mayor vanishes. On the fifth day a man with no past walks out of the ashes, and on the seventh he tells a full stadium that God sent him.

“…till about thirty Dajjals (liars) appear, and each one of them will claim that he is Allah’s Messenger.”

Sahih al-Bukhari 7121
Narration

The fire started at midnight.

By sunrise, city hall was gone.

Three days earlier, it was only a whisper.

The mayor had sold the city's water.

There was no proof. There didn't need to be.

By the second day, a million people filled the streets.

Banners hung from every bridge.

Half the city wanted him gone.

The other half swore he was innocent.

On the third night, someone threw the first flame.

The mayor vanished.

No one knows if he ran… or if he was taken.

For two days, the city had no one.

No water. No bread. No answers.

Then, on the fifth day, a man walked out of the ashes.

No name. No past.

He handed out bread. He spoke softly.

Everything he said sounded true.

By the seventh day, the stadium was full.

And he told them God had sent him.

Half the city fell to its knees.

The Prophet warned that before the Hour, around thirty liars would come, each claiming to be a messenger of God.

The next morning, the neighboring city heard his name.

Caption
The fire started at midnight. By the seventh day, half the city was on its knees.
